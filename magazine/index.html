Editor's Letter

You don't need a lifetime to deserve a memoir. You need one remarkable year.

People write memoirs to tell their story their own way, to keep it before the details fade, and to leave something for the people they love. Most wait decades. The Year in Review does it now, one chapter at a time.

  • Tell it your way.

    Your year, told by you and the people who know you, not by an algorithm.

  • Keep it while it's vivid.

    The details fade fast. We capture the wins, the turning points and the why while they are fresh.

  • Honor who carried you.

    Thank the people who showed up, and remember the ones you lost along the way.

With love, the editors

What clients and their assistants ask us first.

  • Is my story kept private?

    Yes. Your photos and story are private by default and are never shared or published without your written permission.

  • Can my assistant handle everything?

    Yes. One point of contact for your team. Your assistant can send photos, schedule the interview and approve proofs.

  • Do I get revisions?

    Yes. Every tier includes revision rounds, and nothing prints until you approve a final proof.

  • How long does it take?

Questions, answered.

What can I personalize?

Photos, names, messages, and the details that make the story yours, such as captions, favorite quotes, and inside jokes. Each keepsake page lists what can be personalized for that format.

What photos and text should I prepare?

Start by gathering your favorite photos of the person or group, with the clearest, best-lit versions you have. Write down names exactly as they should appear, along with any messages, dates, or memories you want to include. It helps to choose a few favorites to lead with, like a cover photo.

Can I create a magazine as a gift?

Yes. These keepsakes are designed with gifting in mind, whether it is for a birthday, an anniversary, a graduation, or simply someone you want to celebrate.
