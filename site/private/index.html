Editor's letter

People write memoirs to tell their story before someone else does.

They write to be remembered for the right things, to say thank you to the people who carried them, and to make sense of a season while it is still fresh.

You do not have to wait until the end of a career to do that. The Year in Review is a memoir snapshot: one remarkable year, told by you, written by an editor and printed like the magazines on your coffee table.

Your name is already in headlines, credits and programs. None of them were written for the people who know you best. This one is.

From phone screen to feature story

Five steps. About two hours of your time.

  1. Private consultation

    A short call with you or your assistant to agree on the edition, the angle, the deadline and who receives copies.

  2. The interview

    Sixty to ninety minutes with your editor. We prepare the questions; you just talk. Optional short interviews with your circle.

  3. Writing and design

    We write the feature, choose and art direct your photos, and lay out the cover and spreads.

  4. Your proof

    You review a digital proof of the full issue. Nothing prints without your sign-off.

  5. Print and delivery

    Your private run is printed softcover and delivered, ready to give.

Questions

Before you commission.

How is this different from a photo book app?

An app arranges photos you already have. We interview you, write an original feature and design it as a magazine. You are paying for the reporting, writing, art direction and privacy.

How much of my time does it take?

A short consultation, one interview of sixty to ninety minutes, and a review of your proof.

Can it be a gift or a surprise?

Yes. We can interview the people around the recipient and keep the issue a surprise until delivery.

How long does it take?

Your timeline is confirmed at consultation. We are taking only 9 holiday commissions. For printed issues in time for Christmas, commission by December 1.

Is it hardcover?

Launch editions are printed softcover on magazine stock, the way a real issue is printed.
